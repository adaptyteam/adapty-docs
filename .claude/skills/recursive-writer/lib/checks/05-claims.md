# 05 Claims and scope

**Applies when:** only, each, every, all, always, never, any, the first time; a list; a statement about time or the reader's progress.
**Triggers:** \b(only|each|every|all|always|never|any|already|later|still|first time|its|their)\b|\w's\b|^\s*([-*]|\d+\.)\s
**Requires:** every claim, scope word, label, and name holds in every case the reader can hit.
**Evidence:** each absolute or scope word (only, each, every, all, always, never, the first time), and
the edge cases tested (another store or platform, an anonymous user, the else branch, the app already
installed).

- A list or scope noun covers every case; a partial list doesn't present itself as complete. The scope
  is neither narrower nor wider than the real thing. To fix a claim that misses a case, narrow the
  claim first (its subject, its heading) before you add a sentence about the case.
- State the exact boundary of a condition. A temporary constraint isn't stated or implied as permanent.
  A timing claim names its moment, never "right away" or "in advance" with nothing to anchor it; no
  wording implies the wrong timing.
- The premise holds for the target audience. Timeless present tense: no rollout history, no tense that
  presumes the reader's progress ("If you've created…", "installed").
- Contested facts and instruction paths the sources don't confirm end to end: drop them; don't assert
  the opposite; prescribe the verified path.
- A possessive or "the" that presumes something exists ("its text") holds only if every case has it;
  otherwise say "any text on it". A possessive also narrows to the right set ("your products": every
  product in Adapty, or the ones on the screen?).
