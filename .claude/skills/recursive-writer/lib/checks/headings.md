# Headings

**Applies when:** a heading.
**Triggers:** ^#{2,6}\s
**Requires:** the heading's shape follows what the section is.
**Evidence:** the section's kind and the heading's promise.

- Task: the reader's action ("Select languages").
- Reference to one screen object the reader consults: the object's name as the screen shows it, or
  the reader's plain name for that part of the screen. That name is what the reader learns there, so
  the bare-topic rule below doesn't apply to it.
- Product mechanism or concept: the product term plus a plain gloss ("Suppression (removal from
  mailing list)"), or what the reader learns about it. Keep the term; never replace it with a "What…"
  question.
- A section where the reader compares two named things they might confuse or choose between: "X vs. Y"
  is fine ("Adapty analytics vs. store analytics").
- Situation: the moment in plain words ("Product change at the next renewal").
- Container over numbered steps or examples: one plain word ("Setup", "Examples"); the step headings
  under it carry the specifics. The next rule doesn't apply to containers.
- Every heading names what the reader does, decides, or learns there; never the section's layout, a
  bare topic, a state, a vague operation, or "see X". It is specific enough for a table-of-contents
  scanner, promises only what the section covers, doesn't repeat the article title, and takes
  sentence case. Siblings are parallel and visibly related.
