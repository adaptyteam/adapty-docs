# 08 Plain and short

**Applies when:** always.
**Requires:** one idea per sentence, in the shortest plain wording that keeps every fact the reader needs.
**Evidence:** each joint (dash, colon, semicolon, "and", "which") marked "same idea" or "new fact"; the
word count; what was cut.

- A joint is fine when what follows explains, qualifies, or exemplifies the same idea. Split when it
  adds a separate fact, or when the sentence doesn't read in one pass.
- About 15 words; over 20, split or justify. Five or more very short sentences in a row: merge some.
- No posh, literary, or roundabout phrasing; no clever constructions; no idioms.
- No content word or idea twice in a sentence or in adjacent sentences, unless repeating the
  mechanism explains the instruction; no stutters. After a group name, stop repeating the names it
  replaces.
- No stacked prepositions, noun stacks, or stacked modifiers. No sentence ends with a preposition ("the
  placement you added the flow to").
