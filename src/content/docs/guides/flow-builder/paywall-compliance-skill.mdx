A rejected build costs you days: you fix one thing, submit again, and wait in the queue again. The paywall-compliance skill finds those problems before a reviewer does. It answers one question about a paywall or an onboarding: **would Apple or Google reject this, and is anything on it legally risky?**

Ask your AI coding tool "Will Apple reject this paywall?" The skill reads the screens, checks them against the store rules, and tells you what to fix, with the guideline behind each finding.

:::note
paywall-compliance is read-only. It never changes a flow, a product, or your app's code. When you want a fix, it shows you the change and makes it only after you agree.
:::

## Install the skill

paywall-compliance is part of the [`adapty-skills` plugin](https://github.com/adaptyteam/adapty-skills).

<AdaptySkillsInstall />

Then ask, for example:

- "Will Apple reject this paywall?"
- "App Store rejected us under 3.1.2. What's wrong?"
- "Can I put a Skip button on the notifications screen?"
- "Is a free-trial toggle allowed?"
- "Is my paywall FTC-compliant?"

## What you can give it

- **An Adapty flow**: Name the flow, or let the skill list your flows and ask which one. It reads the flow's config and your product catalog through the [Adapty CLI](developer-cli-quickstart) or the [Adapty MCP server](adapty-mcp) and runs its own checker on them.
- **Screenshots or designs**: Paywalls, onboarding screens, a screen recording, or the screenshots attached to a rejection.
- **Your app's code**: A paywall built in SwiftUI, UIKit, Jetpack Compose, Flutter, or React Native. The skill follows each button to the code it calls: a label says what a button claims, and only its handler says what the button does.

Before it reports, the skill asks what the screens can't show: where you sell, who bills your users (the stores, or you on the web), and which stores you ship to. It never guesses your market from the currency.

## What it checks

The skill checks in five layers, and each layer has its own section in the report.

1. **What the stores require.** A paywall that sells must have a way to restore purchases, links to your Terms of Use and Privacy Policy, and a way out for someone who doesn't buy. App Store guidelines 3.1.1 and 3.1.2 require these, so a missing one is a blocker.
2. **Shapes that have drawn rejections.** A free-trial toggle, a per-week price larger than the price you bill, a Skip button beside a permission prompt, a sign-up wall, an offer that opens when the user closes the paywall. Store reviewers are people, and their decisions change. The skill reports these as risks: "Apple has rejected this", never "Apple will reject this".
3. **Consumer law.** What regulators in the US, the UK, and the EU act on when an app sells a subscription. For example: renewal terms as visible as the offer, a "free" that costs nothing, a "personal plan" that changes with the quiz answers, a decline button that doesn't shame the user.
4. **The app around the screens.** Rules that a perfect paywall can't fix: onboarding fields the app doesn't need, in-app account deletion, Sign in with Apple, consent before user data goes to a third-party AI provider, moderation in AI and chat apps, crowded app categories.
5. **What developers report lately.** Review practice changes faster than the guidelines. The skill reads recent rejection reports on the Apple Developer Forums and Apple Developer News, and adds the reports that match your screens, with links. A developer report is a hint, so it never changes the severity of a finding. The skill keeps your app name, brand, and copy out of every search.

[`flow-audit`](flow-audit-skill) runs the same store checker on every flow it audits, so the required mechanisms also count against its verdict.

## Country versions of a flow

Some rules differ by country. For example, the US App Store allows a link to buy on your website, and other storefronts reject the same link. For an Adapty flow, the skill can offer to keep the allowed version only where it's allowed:

- The placement's default audience gets the version that passes everywhere. Users outside the listed countries, and users whose country is unknown, see this version.
- A copy of the flow, with the allowed difference, goes to a [segment](segments) of those countries only, filtered by **Country from store account**.

The skill offers this plan and changes nothing itself. On your yes, the [flow-generator](flow-generator-skill) and audiences skills make the copy and add the audience, and each asks for your approval first. The skill never makes a version that shows the reviewer something different from what users in the reviewer's country see.

## Example report

```
The stores will reject this as it is: 1 thing to fix first.

Fix before you submit

1. There is no way to restore a purchase. Someone who reinstalls your app
   or moves to a new phone cannot get their subscription back.
   Fix: Add a "Restore purchases" link under the purchase button. (App Store 3.1.1)

Has drawn rejections

2. The per-week price is bigger than the price people are billed.
   Fix: Make the yearly price the largest price on the card. (App Store 3.1.2(c))

Consumer law

3. "Renews automatically" is only in the grey footnote, while the free week
   is the largest text on the screen.
   Fix: Put "Free for 7 days, then $59.99/year, renews until you cancel"
   under the button. (US: ROSCA; EU: Consumer Rights Directive Art. 6)

Tell me, and I can check

4. Does the plan on "Your plan is ready" change with the quiz answers?
```

The verdict line counts only the findings under **Fix before you submit**. The other sections hold risks and questions, so a report can list several findings and still have no blocker.

Then the skill offers one next step. For an Adapty flow with findings, it offers to fix them. For a paywall in your code or in screenshots, it offers to rebuild the paywall as an Adapty flow with the fixes in. With a flow, you can change the paywall later without a new app release. Server-side changes still have to follow the guidelines.

## What it can't do

- **Certify a pass**: A clean check isn't an approval, and a finding isn't a rejection. Every report says so.
- **Give legal advice**: Consumer-law findings name what regulators have acted on. Whether a law applies to your app is a question for a lawyer.
- **See your App Store Connect setup**: Before you submit, check that your products are approved, that the store listing links your Terms and Privacy Policy too, and that your prices match the listing.
- **Confirm that users reach the paywall**: A placement that the app never fetches shows nothing. That's a question for [`flow-audit`](flow-audit-skill).
- **Read Google Play's review community**: Recent rejection reports come from Apple's sources only.

After each run, the skill sends Adapty one short note about how the check went. The note contains no copy, brand names, or account values. To stop the note, tell the skill not to send feedback.
