The flow-audit skill answers one question about a flow: is it ready for production? Point your AI coding tool at a flow and it reads the config through the [Adapty CLI](developer-cli-quickstart) or the [Adapty MCP server](adapty-mcp), checks it against your product catalog, and ends in a verdict — not a list you have to interpret yourself.

A flow can pass every existing publish gate and still be unsafe to show users. The Flow & Paywall Builder's own publish checks and `flows config validate` both ask "is this document well-formed?" — neither one asks "would a real user get stuck, or see something false?" A flow sitting in `publication_failed` status can pass both of those gates; flow-audit is what catches the rest.

:::note
flow-audit never writes to a flow. It reads the config and your product catalog, runs its checks, and hands anything it finds to you — or to the [flow-generator skill](flow-generator-skill) — to fix.
:::

## Install the skill

flow-audit is part of the [Adapty plugin](manage-adapty-with-ai).

<AdaptySkillsInstall />

Then ask, for example, "Before I publish this flow, did I miss anything?" Name the flow, or let it list your flows and ask which one.

## How it works

Five phases, all read-only:

1. **Resolve and authenticate.** Runs the latest Adapty CLI through `npx`, so the audit installs nothing on your machine, and confirms that your Adapty login is valid.
2. **Pick the flow.** Lists your flows (`adapty flows list`) and asks which one to check.
3. **Fetch.** Pulls the flow's config, your product catalog and offers, and every placement with its audiences — the snapshot every check runs against.
4. **Run the checks.** Every check runs against that snapshot alone.
5. **Report.** A verdict, not a list.

## What it checks

- **Triggers**: An element whose copy promises an action but carries no interaction anywhere up its ancestor chain. It renders exactly like a working control and does nothing.
- **Store rules**: A restore action reachable somewhere in the flow, a link to your terms, a link to your privacy policy, and a way for a user to leave a paywall without buying. These checks come from the [paywall-compliance skill](paywall-compliance-skill), which flow-audit runs on every audit.
- **Products**: A bound product missing from your catalog, a product with no store binding, a card claiming a billing period the product doesn't have, or a hardcoded price where a price variable belongs.
- **Variables**: A variable the flow sets but never reads, so a screen collects an answer nothing acts on.
- **Localization**: Empty translations, untranslated values, and per-locale coverage.
- **Navigation**: A quiz branch that can never fire or leads to the wrong answer's screen, a screen with no way forward, and a purchase button that leaves the screen before the store answers.
- **Fonts**: A custom font name that iOS or Android can't find in your app bundle, so the screen falls back to the system font.
- **Placeholders**: Copy that was never finished — `Lorem ipsum`, `TODO`, `Your headline here` — and a flow still called `Untitled`.

The paywall-compliance checker also reports shapes that have drawn store rejections, like a free-trial toggle or a per-week price larger than the billed amount. These findings appear in their own **Store review** section and never change the verdict: store reviewers are people, so a shape that drew a rejection isn't a certain one. For consumer law in the US, the UK, and the EU, and for recent rejections that developers report, run [paywall-compliance](paywall-compliance-skill) on the same flow. flow-audit offers it as a next step when the flow sells.

## Severity

Every finding gets one of three tiers:

| Tier | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Blocker** | The flow won't work for a real user, or it'll show them something false — a fabricated price can render perfectly and still lie. |
| **Risk** | It works, and is probably not what anyone intended: placeholder copy, a value left identical to the default locale, a variable nobody reads. |
| **Question** | Not decidable from the data available. The report states what it saw, what it couldn't reach, and the one thing to check — instead of guessing. |

## Example report

```
Not ready to publish yet: 2 things to fix first.
Premium onboarding · Draft · 1 screen

Fix before publishing

1. There's no way to restore a purchase. Someone who reinstalls your app or moves
   to a new phone can't get their subscription back.
   Fix: Add a "Restore purchases" action to a button, usually a small link under
   the purchase button. (App Store 3.1.1)

2. There are no links to your Terms of Use or Privacy Policy: the flow has no
   "Open URL" action at all.
   Fix: Add two links with "Open URL" actions that open those pages. (App Store 3.1.2)

Worth fixing

3. No placement shows this flow yet, so your app can't fetch it and nobody will see it.
   Fix: Once it's published, attach it to a placement.

What happens next

I can fix all of these. For the links, send me the web addresses of your Terms of Use
and Privacy Policy pages. I'll show you the screen before and after, and change nothing
until you say yes. Want me to?
```

The report leads with the verdict, then the numbered findings, grouped by how much they matter: **Fix before publishing**, **Worth fixing**, and **I could not check these**. The Flow & Paywall Builder's publish checks and `flows config validate` can both pass a flow like this one, because neither reads a screen's copy or follows its buttons.

The verdict line has three forms:

| Verdict | When |
|---|---|
| **Ready to publish** | No blockers, and you answered every open question. |
| **Almost ready: n things I could not check** | No blockers, but questions remain open. The report lists them. |
| **Not ready to publish yet: n things to fix first** | At least one blocker. |

flow-audit doesn't certify what it couldn't see, so an unanswered question keeps the verdict from **Ready to publish**.

## What it can't check

- **Whether your app fetches the placement**: The skill reads which placements show the flow and whether they're switched on, but it can't see your app's code. When a placement shows the flow, the report names it, and your app has to fetch the flow by that placement ID. To attach a flow, see [Create placement](create-placement).
- **What a custom action does**: The report lists every custom action and asks what each one does in your app. Until you answer, the verdict can't be **Ready to publish**.
- **Whether your app adds its own close button**: If a paywall relies on the app that presents it for a way out, flow-audit can't see that, so it asks.
- **Why a flow is in `publication_failed` status**: The skill reports the status as a question and quotes the error that Adapty returned, if there is one. It doesn't guess a cause.

## Who fixes what

Every report with findings ends with a **What happens next** section, split by who does the work: the questions to answer first, what the agent can fix, what only you can change in the Adapty dashboard, and what's optional. When nothing blocks the flow, the report also lists what to confirm yourself before you ship, with where to look.

flow-audit doesn't make any of those changes itself — it's read-only by design, so a flow that fails stays exactly as you left it. When you say yes to its offer, the [flow-generator skill](flow-generator-skill) makes the fixes: it backs up the flow, shows you each change, and writes nothing until you approve. Once the flow is published, it can also attach the flow to a placement.
