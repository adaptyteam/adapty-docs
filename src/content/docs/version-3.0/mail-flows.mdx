A **flow** turns a saved campaign into scheduled deliveries. It sends a campaign or an A/B test to one **segment** of your users. The **purpose** of a flow is the situation its emails are written for, such as an expired subscription. Each purpose has an [entry rule](mail-purposes#entry-rules-events-and-conditions-that-activate-a-purpose): one or more events, such as a failed payment or a new profile. The entry rule can also include a condition that users must meet. A user matches a purpose when they trigger one of its entry events. You don't launch anything by hand.

:::note
Adapty's core product has its own flows. They're a different feature — nothing here applies to them.
:::

The **Flows** page has a **Marketing** and a **Transactional** tab. Each tab lists the flows of that kind, one per row, grouped by purpose. Within each purpose, the flows appear in order of priority. Each row shows the flow's content, purpose, priority, and segment, then **Sent**, your project's first three [rates](mail-analytics#change-the-rates), and **Revenue** for the content the flow sends now. To find a flow, search, or filter by purpose.

## Purposes

Each flow has one purpose. The purpose decides what starts the flow and which campaigns it can send: a flow offers only the campaigns written for its purpose. Every project starts with eight purposes. On the **Purposes** page, you can [change their rules and add purposes](mail-purposes).

### Marketing purposes

Every project starts with five marketing purposes. Their flows are on the **Marketing** tab of the **Flows** page. Each purpose is named after the subscription state that its default condition requires:

- **Never purchased**: Users who signed up but haven't subscribed or made a one-time purchase yet. Goal: activation and first conversion. Trial users aren't here — starting a trial counts as an active subscription.
- **Renewal cancelled**: Users who turned off auto-renewal but still have an active subscription. Covers both paid subscribers and trial users who cancelled before conversion. Strongest window to save them — they still have access. Split paid and trial audiences via segment filters if messaging needs to differ.
- **Expired**: Subscription has lapsed and access is gone. Covers both paid expirations and trials that ran out without converting. Goal: win them back. Segment filters can tailor copy for trial-expired and paid-expired audiences.
- **Billing issue**: Payment failed — declined or expired card, or grace period after a missed renewal. Goal: urgent, helpful recovery, not a sales push. Recover them quickly — they already wanted to pay.
- **Refunded**: Users who received a refund for a subscription. Goal: understand what went wrong and offer a better-fit option. Tone should stay humble and curious, not a hard resell.

The [exit rule](mail-purposes#exit-rules) of a purpose decides what ends its sequences early. Whatever the exit rule, a marketing sequence ends early when the user [opens and clicks none of its emails](mail-suppression#no-engagement-an-ignored-marketing-sequence-ends-early).

### Transactional purposes

Transactional flows carry service messages. Every project starts with three transactional purposes. Their flows are on the **Transactional** tab of the **Flows** page:

- **Double opt-in**: Starts when a new profile with marketing consent arrives from your [entry point](mail-entry-points). It stops as soon as the address is confirmed. [Turning on double opt-in](mail-double-opt-in) creates its flow, and turning it off deletes every flow of this purpose. You can't change or delete this purpose: the double opt-in setting relies on it.
- **App activation**: Starts with a purchase and gets the buyer into your app. The button in the email should be a deep link: it installs the app if needed and signs the buyer in.
- **Cancellation page**: Starts with a purchase and sends a new subscriber, once, the page where they review, change, or cancel the subscription.

Both purchase purposes start on the same purchase, so a buyer who is in the segments of an **App activation** flow and a **Cancellation page** flow receives both. Transactional flows run alongside any applicable marketing flows.

By default, the [re-entry rule](mail-purposes#re-entry-rules-when-the-purpose-can-apply-again) of **Double opt-in**, **App activation**, and **Cancellation page** is **Never**. A profile receives a sequence from each of them at most once, even after you change what their flows send. To let profiles receive a sequence from **App activation** or **Cancellation page** again, change the re-entry rule of the purpose.

:::note
By default, renewals don't start **App activation** or **Cancellation page** flows. To send an email on each renewal, [add a transactional purpose](mail-purposes#create-a-purpose) and select **Subscription renewed** as an entry event. Then [create a flow](mail-create-flow#create-a-flow) for the purpose.
:::

## Audience and content

A flow pairs a **user segment** with what it receives. A purpose can have several flows, one for each segment, so one purpose can send different content to different segments.

A typical setup starts with one flow for each purpose you write for, sending to **All Users**. When an audience needs different copy, such as paid users in one country, add a flow for its segment and move it before **All Users**, which then catches everyone else.

### Who qualifies

A [segment](mail-segments) is a set of filters over your user base. A flow only sends emails to users in its segment. You don't need a segment for each country: in a [localized campaign](mail-localization), Adapty Mail selects the language for each recipient.

**All Users** is a built-in segment with no filters, so everyone qualifies. You can't edit or delete it.

### What they receive

Each flow sends one of two kinds of content:

- **Campaign**: Everyone in the segment gets the same campaign.
- **A/B Test**: Two or more campaigns with configurable weights. Adapty Mail routes each user to one of them at random. To pick a winner, [compare the campaigns in Analytics](mail-ab-testing#read-results).

### Priority

When a user is in the segments of several flows of one purpose, **priority** decides which flow they enter. The **Priority** column of the **Flows** page shows it. When a user matches the purpose, Adapty Mail:

1. Evaluates the flows of the purpose in order of priority, from 1 up.
2. Starts the first flow with the user in its segment.
3. Stops. The flows after it are not evaluated for that user.

A flow with a broad segment, placed before a flow with a narrower segment, takes every user in both segments.

A new flow goes after the other numbered flows of its purpose. A flow with **All Users** shows **Last** in the **Priority** column and always stays last, because every user is in its segment. To change the order, on the **Flows** page, click [**Change order**](mail-create-flow#change-the-order-of-flows).

Priority works within one purpose. Across purposes, a user is in one marketing flow at a time. When several marketing purposes share an entry event, their [order on the **Purposes** page](mail-purposes#change-the-order-of-marketing-purposes) decides from which purpose the user receives a sequence.

## Flow lifecycle

Flows have no draft state. They go live the moment you create them. From then on, a user in the segment who matches the purpose joins the campaign immediately, unless:

- They're already in a marketing sequence.
- They also match a marketing purpose that comes earlier in the **Priority** order.
- The re-entry rule of the purpose doesn't allow them a new sequence.

The first email follows after its **Delay**.

How an edit takes effect depends on what you change:

- **Flow edits**: A flow edit only applies to users who enter the flow afterwards. Deleting a flow doesn't cancel emails already scheduled.
- **Campaign edits**: After you add a campaign to a flow, you can change only its name and description, and [add languages](mail-localization). Its emails and **Email Destination** are locked. To change them, duplicate the campaign, edit the copy, and select the copy as the flow's **Campaign**. Users already in the original campaign's sequence keep receiving its emails.
- **Purpose edits**: A change to the rules of a purpose applies to every flow of the purpose, and to the sequences already running.

You can't change the purpose or the segment of a flow. To send to another segment, [replace the flow](mail-create-flow#send-a-flow-to-another-segment).

An [A/B test](mail-ab-testing#lifecycle) has a lifecycle of its own: **draft**, **live**, then **finished**.

### Email delivery timing

- Each email has its own **Delay** — how long after the previous one it goes out. You can edit them in the campaign editor while the campaign is a draft.
- Adapty Mail only sends between 08:00 and 21:00 in the recipient's timezone. A send due outside those hours shifts to the nearest edge of the window.
- The first email is exempt when it's due within two hours of the entry event. It goes out on time, even at night.
