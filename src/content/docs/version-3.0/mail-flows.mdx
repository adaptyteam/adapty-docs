A **flow** turns a saved campaign into scheduled deliveries. It sends a campaign or an A/B test to one **segment** of your users at one moment of the customer lifecycle: the flow's **purpose**. A **trigger** — an event in the user's subscription history — starts the flow. Adapty Mail evaluates the flows whenever a matching event fires — no polling, no cron, no manual launch.

:::note
Adapty's core product has its own flows. They're a different feature — nothing here applies to them.
:::

The **Flows** page lists every flow of the project, one per row, with its content, purpose, priority, segment, and sending stats.

## Purposes

Each flow has one purpose. The purpose decides which trigger starts the flow and which campaigns it can send: a flow offers only the campaigns written for its purpose. Purposes are fixed — you can't create your own or extend the list.

### Marketing purposes

Adapty Mail has five marketing purposes, on the **Marketing** tab of the **Flows** page. Each one is named after the subscription state that starts it:

- **Never purchased**: Users who signed up but haven't made a purchase yet. Goal: activation and first conversion. Trial users aren't here — starting a trial counts as an active subscription.
- **Renewal cancelled**: Users who turned off auto-renewal but still have an active subscription. Covers both paid subscribers and trial users who cancelled before conversion. Strongest window to save them — they still have access. Split paid and trial audiences via segment filters if messaging needs to differ.
- **Billing issue**: Payment failed — declined or expired card, or grace period after a missed renewal. Goal: urgent, helpful recovery, not a sales push. Recover them quickly — they already wanted to pay.
- **Expired**: Subscription has lapsed and access is gone. Covers both paid expirations and trials that ran out without converting. Goal: win them back. Segment filters can tailor copy for trial-expired and paid-expired audiences.
- **Refunded**: Users who requested a refund after purchase. Goal: understand what went wrong and offer a better-fit option. Tone should stay humble and curious, not a hard resell.

A marketing flow runs one at a time per recipient: while one sequence is underway, no second one starts. It ends when they buy, or early when they [open and click none of its emails](mail-suppression#no-engagement-an-ignored-marketing-campaign-ends-early).

### Transactional purposes

Transactional flows carry service messages. They have three purposes, on the **Transactional** tab of the **Flows** page:

- **Double opt-in**: Starts when a new profile arrives from your [entry point](mail-entry-points), while [double opt-in](mail-double-opt-in) is on. It stops as soon as the address is confirmed.
- **App activation**: Starts with a purchase, and gets the buyer into your app.
- **Cancellation page**: Starts with a purchase, and hands the buyer the subscription controls.

Both purchase purposes start on the initial purchase of a product, so a buyer who matches flows of both gets both. Each transactional flow runs once per profile, alongside any applicable marketing flows.

:::note
Renewals don't start these flows.
:::

## Audience and content

A flow pairs a **user segment** with what it receives. A purpose can have several flows, one for each segment, so one moment can serve several audiences.

### Who qualifies

A segment is a set of filters over your user base — see [Segments](mail-segments). Only users that match the segment enter the flow. You don't need a segment for each country: in a [localized campaign](mail-localization), Adapty Mail selects the language for each recipient.

**All Users** is a built-in segment with no filters, so everyone qualifies. You can't edit or delete it.

### What they receive

Each flow sends one of two kinds of content:

- **Campaign**: Everyone who matches gets the same campaign.
- **A/B Test**: Two or more campaigns with configurable weights. Adapty Mail routes users across them at random and tracks metrics per variant — see [A/B testing](mail-ab-testing).

### Priority

When several flows of a purpose could match the same person, **priority** decides which one they enter. The **Priority** column of the **Flows** page shows it. When a user hits the trigger, Adapty Mail:

1. Evaluates the flows of the purpose in order of priority, from 1 up.
2. Starts the first flow whose segment the user matches.
3. Stops. The flows after it are not evaluated for that user.

A broad segment placed above a narrower one swallows every user who would otherwise match the narrower one.

A new flow goes after the other flows of its purpose. To change the order, see [Change the order of flows](mail-create-flow#change-the-order-of-flows).

:::important
A flow with **All Users** has no number and always goes last, because it matches every user.
:::

## Flow lifecycle

Flows have no draft state. They go live the moment you create them. A user who hits the trigger and matches the segment joins the campaign immediately, and the first email follows after its **Delay**.

How an edit takes effect depends on what you change:

- **Flow edits**: A flow edit only applies to users who hit the trigger afterwards. Deleting a flow doesn't cancel emails already scheduled.
- **Campaign edits**: After you add a campaign to a flow, you can change only its name and description. Its emails, languages, and **Email Destination** are locked. To change them, duplicate the campaign, edit the copy, and put the copy on the flow. Users already in the original campaign's sequence keep receiving its emails.

The purpose and the segment of a flow don't change. To send to another segment, delete the flow and create another one.

An A/B test has a lifecycle of its own — **draft**, **live**, then **finished**. See [A/B testing](mail-ab-testing).

### Email delivery timing

- Each email has its own **Delay** — how long after the previous one it goes out. You can edit them in the campaign editor while the campaign is a draft.
- Adapty Mail only sends between 08:00 and 21:00 in the recipient's timezone. A send due outside those hours shifts to the nearest edge of the window.
- The first email is exempt when it's due within two hours of the trigger. It goes out on time, even at night.
