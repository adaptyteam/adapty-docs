A **flow** turns a saved campaign into scheduled deliveries. It sends a campaign or an A/B test to one **segment** of your users at one moment of the customer lifecycle: the flow's **purpose**. Each purpose has its own **trigger**. For a marketing purpose, the trigger is the user's subscription state, such as an expired subscription or a failed payment. For a transactional purpose, it's a moment, such as a new profile or a first purchase. Adapty Mail evaluates the flows whenever an event about the user arrives. You don't launch anything by hand.

:::note
Adapty's core product has its own flows. They're a different feature — nothing here applies to them.
:::

The **Flows** page has a **Marketing** and a **Transactional** tab. Each tab lists the flows of that kind, one per row, grouped by purpose in order of priority. Each row shows the flow's content, purpose, priority, and segment, then **Sent**, your project's first three [rates](mail-analytics#change-the-rates), and **Revenue** for the content the flow sends now. To find a flow, search, or filter by purpose.

## Purposes

Each flow has one purpose. The purpose decides which trigger starts the flow and which campaigns it can send: a flow offers only the campaigns written for its purpose. Purposes are fixed — you can't create your own or extend the list.

### Marketing purposes

Adapty Mail has five marketing purposes. Their flows are on the **Marketing** tab of the **Flows** page. Each purpose is named after the subscription state of the users it reaches:

- **Never purchased**: Users who signed up but haven't made a purchase yet. Goal: activation and first conversion. Trial users aren't here — starting a trial counts as an active subscription.
- **Renewal cancelled**: Users who turned off auto-renewal but still have an active subscription. Covers both paid subscribers and trial users who cancelled before conversion. Strongest window to save them — they still have access. Split paid and trial audiences via segment filters if messaging needs to differ.
- **Expired**: Subscription has lapsed and access is gone. Covers both paid expirations and trials that ran out without converting. Goal: win them back. Segment filters can tailor copy for trial-expired and paid-expired audiences.
- **Billing issue**: Payment failed — declined or expired card, or grace period after a missed renewal. Goal: urgent, helpful recovery, not a sales push. Recover them quickly — they already wanted to pay.
- **Refunded**: Users who requested a refund after purchase. Goal: understand what went wrong and offer a better-fit option. Tone should stay humble and curious, not a hard resell.

A marketing flow runs one at a time per recipient: while one sequence is underway, no second one starts. It ends when they buy, or early when they [open and click none of its emails](mail-suppression#no-engagement-an-ignored-marketing-campaign-ends-early).

### Transactional purposes

Transactional flows carry service messages. They have three purposes. Their flows are on the **Transactional** tab of the **Flows** page:

- **Double opt-in**: Starts when a new profile with marketing consent arrives from your [entry point](mail-entry-points). It stops as soon as the address is confirmed. [Turning on double opt-in](mail-double-opt-in) creates its flow, and turning it off deletes every flow of this purpose.
- **App activation**: Starts with a purchase and gets the buyer into your app. The email's button is a deep link: it installs the app if needed and signs the buyer in.
- **Cancellation page**: Starts with a purchase and sends a new subscriber, once, the page where they review, change, or cancel the subscription.

Both purchase purposes start on the initial purchase of a product, so a buyer who is in the segments of an **App activation** flow and a **Cancellation page** flow gets both. A profile gets each transactional purpose once: from the first flow of it whose segment they're in, and never again, even after you change the flow's content. Transactional flows run alongside any applicable marketing flows.

:::note
Renewals don't start these flows.
:::

## Audience and content

A flow pairs a **user segment** with what it receives. A purpose can have several flows, one for each segment, so one purpose can send different content to different segments.

A typical setup starts with one flow for each purpose you write for, sending to **All Users**. When an audience needs different copy, such as paid users in one country, add a flow for its segment and move it before **All Users**, which then catches everyone else.

### Who qualifies

A segment is a set of filters over your user base — see [Segments](mail-segments). Only users that match the segment enter the flow. You don't need a segment for each country: in a [localized campaign](mail-localization), Adapty Mail selects the language for each recipient.

**All Users** is a built-in segment with no filters, so everyone qualifies. You can't edit or delete it.

### What they receive

Each flow sends one of two kinds of content:

- **Campaign**: Everyone in the segment gets the same campaign.
- **A/B Test**: Two or more campaigns with configurable weights. Adapty Mail routes users across them at random and tracks metrics per variant — see [A/B testing](mail-ab-testing).

### Priority

When a user is in the segments of several flows of one purpose, **priority** decides which flow they enter. The **Priority** column of the **Flows** page shows it. When a user hits the trigger, Adapty Mail:

1. Evaluates the flows of the purpose in order of priority, from 1 up.
2. Starts the first flow whose segment the user matches.
3. Stops. The flows after it are not evaluated for that user.

A flow with a broad segment, placed before a flow with a narrower one, takes every user who would otherwise enter the narrower one.

A new flow goes after the other numbered flows of its purpose. A flow with **All Users** shows **Last** in the **Priority** column and always stays last, because every user is in its segment. To change the order, see [Change the order of flows](mail-create-flow#change-the-order-of-flows).

Priority works within one purpose. Across purposes, a user is in one marketing flow at a time. See [Marketing purposes](#marketing-purposes).

## Flow lifecycle

Flows have no draft state. They go live the moment you create them. A user who hits the trigger and is in the segment joins the campaign immediately, and the first email follows after its **Delay**.

How an edit takes effect depends on what you change:

- **Flow edits**: A flow edit only applies to users who hit the trigger afterwards. Deleting a flow doesn't cancel emails already scheduled.
- **Campaign edits**: After you add a campaign to a flow, you can change only its name and description, and [add languages](mail-localization). Its emails and **Email Destination** are locked. To change them, duplicate the campaign, edit the copy, and select the copy as the flow's **Campaign**. Users already in the original campaign's sequence keep receiving its emails.

You can't change the purpose or the segment of a flow. To send to another segment, [replace the flow](mail-create-flow#send-a-flow-to-another-segment).

An A/B test has a lifecycle of its own — **draft**, **live**, then **finished**. See [A/B testing](mail-ab-testing).

### Email delivery timing

- Each email has its own **Delay** — how long after the previous one it goes out. You can edit them in the campaign editor while the campaign is a draft.
- Adapty Mail only sends between 08:00 and 21:00 in the recipient's timezone. A send due outside those hours shifts to the nearest edge of the window.
- The first email is exempt when it's due within two hours of the trigger. It goes out on time, even at night.
