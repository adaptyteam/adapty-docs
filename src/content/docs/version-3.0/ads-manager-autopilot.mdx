Ads Autopilot raises and lowers your Apple Ads keyword bids and campaign budgets, so that paying users stay within a target cost. Bids and budgets each have their own autopilot:

- **Keyword autopilot**: Sets the bids of the keywords you add to it, and pauses keywords that spend without paying users.
- **Budget autopilot**: Every Monday, moves daily budget from campaigns where paying users cost more to campaigns where they cost less. Its spend guard pauses campaigns that spend without trials or payments.

You can review each change and its reason in the **Autopilot** section of Ads Manager. In **Preview** mode, the budget autopilot only lists its changes there. In **Live** mode, it sends them to Apple Ads. You can stop Ads Autopilot for a single campaign or keyword.

To connect Ads Autopilot for your company, contact your Adapty manager. To add keywords to the keyword autopilot, use the [Adapty CLI](developer-cli-ads-manager#add-keywords-to-the-keyword-autopilot). You can't add keywords in the **Autopilot** section.

:::tip
Through the same CLI, scripts and AI agents can read what Ads Autopilot changed and edit its settings.
:::

## What Ads Autopilot changes in Apple Ads

The keyword autopilot and the budget autopilot both use a target cost per paying user: the highest Apple Ads spend per paying user that Ads Autopilot accepts. Ads Autopilot counts paying users from the purchase data that Adapty collects for your app.

On a keyword or campaign where paying users cost less than the target, Ads Autopilot spends more to reach more of them. Where they cost more, it spends less.

Ads Autopilot changes keyword bids and daily budgets, and it can pause keywords and campaigns. It doesn't add keywords, edit match types or negative keywords, or create campaigns.

### How the keyword autopilot sets bids

Add each keyword to the keyword autopilot separately, in the [Adapty CLI](developer-cli-ads-manager#add-keywords-to-the-keyword-autopilot). For each keyword, set a target cost per paying user. Four times a day, the keyword autopilot reviews these keywords, and no others.

Each keyword moves through the following statuses:

- **Warming up**: The keyword autopilot raises the bid step by step, up to a max bid, until the keyword receives impressions. Every keyword starts here.
- **Testing**: The keyword autopilot counts taps, installs, trials, and payments to judge the keyword.
- **Optimizing**: Payments confirmed that the keyword meets the target. The keyword autopilot keeps the bid at the level where the cost per paying user meets the target.

The keyword autopilot calculates the max bid, unless you set it when you add the keyword. The max bid only applies to **Warming up** and **Testing**, so the keyword autopilot can bid above it for an optimizing keyword.

Instead of a lower bid, the keyword autopilot pauses a keyword in Apple Ads in the following cases:

- The keyword spends without results at one funnel step: taps without installs, installs without trials, or trials without payments.
- The early signals of the keyword predict a cost per paying user far above the target.
- Paying users from an optimizing keyword became more expensive, and a lower bid didn't help.
- The keyword receives too little traffic to judge.

In the **Autopilot** section, the keyword then shows **Paused by autopilot**, with the reason, such as **No installs** or **Trials don't convert**.

### How the budget autopilot moves budgets

Every Monday, the budget autopilot reviews the campaigns that meet all of the following conditions:

- The campaign targets a single country.
- The campaign has a daily budget.
- The [campaign type](ads-manager-create-campaign) is **Generic** or **Competitor**.

For each of these campaigns, the Monday run can propose a new budget. The target cost per paying user of these campaigns comes from the 180-day LTV of your app. To keep the budget of a campaign as it is, [exclude the campaign](#stop-ads-autopilot-for-a-campaign-or-keyword).

The new budgets move money from campaigns where paying users cost more to campaigns where they cost less. In one run, a campaign loses at most 12% of its budget to other campaigns. When paying users in a campaign cost far more than the target, the budget of that campaign shrinks step by step over several runs.

Every 6 hours, the spend guard pauses campaigns that spend without trials or payments.

The budget autopilot starts in **Preview** mode. From its first Monday run, it shows its decisions in the **Autopilot** section, but sends nothing to Apple Ads. To let it change budgets in Apple Ads, [switch to **Live**](#start-or-stop-budget-changes-in-apple-ads).

## Review what Ads Autopilot changed

To see what Ads Autopilot changed and why, click **Autopilot** in the Ads Manager sidebar. The **Autopilot** item appears after Adapty connects the budget autopilot for your company, or after you add the first keyword to the keyword autopilot.

The **Autopilot** section groups its data into the following tabs:

- **Activity**: A feed of the changes that Ads Autopilot made or planned, newest first. Each entry shows what Ads Autopilot saw, the reason for its decision, and the decision. The status of the entry, such as **Applied in Apple Ads** or **Preview · not sent**, shows whether the change reached Apple Ads.
- **Reports**: Summary cards, including the following:
  - **Goal**: How many campaigns meet the target.
  - **Safety**: The campaigns that the spend guard paused.
  - **Needs you**: The campaigns and keywords that wait for your action.
- **Campaigns**: A table of the campaigns that the budget autopilot reviews, with the status, monthly budget, and cost per paying user against the target of each. Excluded campaigns appear in a separate **Not managed** list.
- **Keywords**: A table of the keywords you added to the keyword autopilot, with the status, bid, max bid, target, and last decision of each.
- **Tasks**: Lists of the changes that Ads Autopilot is sending to Apple Ads, the next scheduled runs, and the keywords and campaigns that Ads Autopilot tests or reduces.

A keyword shows **Waiting for review** when Apple Ads doesn't accept a change from the keyword autopilot. The keyword autopilot then stops changing the keyword. To see which change Apple Ads didn't accept, find the keyword on the **Activity** tab. To have the keyword autopilot set the bid of the keyword again, open the **Keywords** tab and find the keyword. In its row, click **Take off**, and then **Return**. The keyword then starts again from **Warming up**.

## Start or stop budget changes in Apple Ads

In **Preview** mode, the budget autopilot calculates its Monday decisions and runs its spend guard checks, but sends nothing to Apple Ads. Before you switch to **Live**, review the decisions of the budget autopilot in the following places:

- **Activity** tab: The budget changes that the last Monday run calculated, with the status **Preview · not sent**.
- **Reports** tab: The **Safety** card, with the campaigns that the spend guard would have paused.
- **Campaigns** tab: The campaigns that the budget autopilot reviews. To keep the budget of a campaign as it is, [exclude the campaign](#stop-ads-autopilot-for-a-campaign-or-keyword).

To let the budget autopilot change budgets in Apple Ads, select **Live** in the **Budget** controls of the **Autopilot** section. If the budget autopilot is off, turn on its switch before you select **Live**. If the controls show **Bids only**, the budget autopilot isn't connected for your company. To connect it, contact your Adapty manager.

Budget changes start with the next Monday run. The budget autopilot doesn't send the decisions it made in **Preview** mode. The spend guard can pause campaigns from its next check, within 6 hours.

Stop budget changes in Apple Ads in one of the following ways:

- **All campaigns, decisions shown**: Select **Preview**. The spend guard then only reports the campaigns it would pause.
- **One campaign**: [Exclude the campaign](#stop-ads-autopilot-for-a-campaign-or-keyword).
- **All campaigns, no decisions**: Turn off the switch of the budget autopilot. The weekly runs and the spend guard then stop.

After you switch to **Preview** or turn the budget autopilot off, the budget autopilot doesn't send its open decisions. Budgets stay as they are, and campaigns that the spend guard paused stay paused. To restore a previous budget, find it on the **Activity** tab and set it yourself.

Keyword bids don't depend on these settings, and the keyword autopilot has no switch of its own. To stop bid changes for a keyword, [stop Ads Autopilot for it](#stop-ads-autopilot-for-a-campaign-or-keyword).

## Stop Ads Autopilot for a campaign or keyword

To stop Ads Autopilot for one campaign, open the **Campaigns** tab of the **Autopilot** section. Find the campaign, and click **Exclude** in its row. The campaign keeps its current budget. The budget autopilot cancels its planned changes for the campaign, but doesn't undo the changes that already reached Apple Ads. The spend guard no longer pauses the campaign.

To return the campaign, find it in the **Not managed** list on the same tab, and click **Return to autopilot**. The next Monday run reviews the campaign again, with its current budget as the new starting point.

To stop Ads Autopilot for one keyword, open the **Keywords** tab. Find the keyword, and click **Take off** in its row. The keyword autopilot stops changing the keyword, but doesn't undo the changes that already reached Apple Ads.

To return the keyword, click **Return** in its row. The keyword autopilot reuses the previous target and max bid, and the keyword starts again from **Warming up**. To return the keyword with a new target or max bid, run [`keyword-autopilot unenroll`, then `enroll`](developer-cli-ads-manager-reference#adapty-asa-keyword-autopilot-enroll) with the new values in the CLI.

## Edit campaigns and keywords outside Ads Autopilot

To see which campaigns the budget autopilot reviews when you edit them in Ads Manager, add the **Managed by** column to the [campaigns table](adapty-ads-manager-analytics#customize-which-metrics-to-show).

Ads Autopilot responds to your own changes in the following ways:

- **Keyword bid**: Within a day, the keyword autopilot uses your bid as its new starting point.
- **Keyword status**: When you pause a keyword or its ad group, the keyword autopilot stops changing the keyword. The keyword then shows **Paused in Apple Ads**. To have the keyword autopilot change it again after you resume it, run [`keyword-autopilot unenroll`, then `enroll`](developer-cli-ads-manager-reference#keyword-autopilot) in the CLI.
- **Campaign budget or status**: In **Live** mode, the budget autopilot excludes a campaign when you change its daily budget or status. Before you [return the campaign](#stop-ads-autopilot-for-a-campaign-or-keyword), make sure no other person or tool changes it.

[Campaign rules](ads-manager-automations-campaign-rules) don't apply to the campaigns controlled by the budget autopilot.

## Resume keywords and campaigns that Ads Autopilot paused

To run a paused keyword or campaign again, resume it in Ads Manager or in Apple Ads. Ads Autopilot never resumes a keyword or campaign that it paused.

The keyword autopilot doesn't set the bid of a keyword that it paused, even after you resume the keyword. To have the keyword autopilot set the bid again, open the **Keywords** tab of the **Autopilot** section and find the keyword. In its row, click **Take off**, and then **Return**. The keyword autopilot starts **Warming up** within a day after the keyword is active. Until then, the keyword shows **Starting**.
